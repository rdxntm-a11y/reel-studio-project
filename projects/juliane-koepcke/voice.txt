She fell three kilometres—and survived. On Christmas Eve 1971, 17-year-old Juliane Koepcke was flying over Peru when lightning struck her plane. The aircraft broke apart, and she woke the next day alone in the rainforest, with a broken collarbone and deep cuts. She had one sandal, no glasses, and only a bag of sweets. So she followed a stream, using what her parents had taught her at their jungle research station. On day four, she found crash victims. Rescue planes flew overhead, but the canopy hid her. Ten days in, weak and starving, she found a hut, treated a maggot-infested wound with petrol, and pulled out about thirty maggots. The next day, local men found her. The twist?
