I ran the Sahara, and it swallowed me.
1994. The Marathon des Sables. 250 kilometres of Morocco.
On day four, an eight-hour sandstorm wiped out the route. I kept moving. That is how I got lost.
Half a bottle of water. Then nothing.
I drank my own urine.
Two days later, I drank bats' blood in a desert shrine.
A rescue helicopter passed overhead. It did not see me.
So I cut my wrists. Nothing came out. The heat had thickened my blood. It would not drain.
I woke up alive.
On day eight, I found an oasis. A shepherd girl led me to a nomad camp in Algeria.
291 kilometres off course. 16 kilograms lighter. Ten days lost. One failed escape.
